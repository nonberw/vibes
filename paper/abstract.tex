\begin{abstract}
Video world models predict futures that look right and obey physics in distribution, then fail when
a physical parameter leaves the training range. We ask \emph{where} such a failure lives. In a
controlled 2D world with per-episode gravity, restitution and friction, where materials are visible
and gravity must be inferred from motion, we train three families of chunk world models that share
one dynamics architecture and differ only in their training signal: pixel reconstruction, latent
prediction (JEPA) and ground-truth positions. We first show that the Bayes-optimal squared-loss
predictor's implied gravity is the posterior mean, which lies inside the training range for every
context, so ``snapping back'' is what the loss asks for, and that low loss alone makes physical
quantities decodable, so probes are weak evidence. We then dissect the failure with matched-state
counterfactuals from time-reversed simulation, belief steering, and nested inversion with physics
measured on held-out frames and an anti-physics control. The pre-registered study gives a mixed
answer, and we report it as such. Of seventeen confirmatory tests four survive correction; the
pre-registered curvature estimator is too noisy out of range to decide between clamp and law, and
the pre-registered locus rule returns no stable label. What is measured tightly: every family's
belief tracks out-of-range gravity but under-reads it by about half (probe slope \StateInfHi{} for
the ground-truth family), and perception is not at fault. In exploratory analyses, predictions sit
about half-way between clamp and law above the range and near the clamp below it. The failure is
split: writing the true gravity into the belief recovers the level of a model trained on the wide
range above the training range for the one family where the write is valid, while below the range
even an optimized belief closes only about half of the gap to that model. More data lowered in-range
error by two pixels per decade and, contrary to our hypothesis, did not leave extrapolation
unchanged. A first version of the study whose models never became competent is reported too. Code,
data and both pre-registrations are at \url{https://github.com/nonberw/vibes}.
\end{abstract}
