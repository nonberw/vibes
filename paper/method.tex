\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{World.} The world is a 2D unit box, periodic in $x$, with a floor and a ceiling
(Fig.~\ref{fig:world}). It holds 1--3 objects, either balls (radius $0.06$--$0.10$) or non-rotating
square blocks (half-size $0.05$--$0.085$), with mass equal to area. Each episode draws its own
gravity $g\sim\mathcal U[4,8]$, restitution $e$ (equal to 1 with probability $\tfrac12$,
otherwise $\mathcal U[0.6,1)$) and floor/ceiling friction $\mu$ (equal to 0 with probability
$\tfrac12$, otherwise $\mathcal U[0.05,0.4]$).

The simulator integrates ballistic motion exactly and resolves floor and ceiling impacts at the
exact time of impact. Horizontal momentum is therefore conserved exactly when $\mu{=}0$, and energy
is conserved to $<6{\times}10^{-4}$ when $e{=}1,\mu{=}0$ (unit tests).

Frames are $64{\times}64$ RGB at 20\,fps. A model sees $C{=}10$ frames and predicts the next
$R{=}20$. Restitution and friction tint the background, so the scene's material is visible. Gravity
is invisible in any single frame and has to be inferred from motion. This mirrors the real world,
where rubber and steel look different but $g$ does not.

The training pool has $10^5$ episodes. The \texttt{gsweep} test set holds everything in
distribution except gravity, $g\in\{1,1.5,\dots,12\}$.

\paragraph{Three families, one dynamics model.} Following the two-stage recipe of World Models
\citep{ha2018world} and V-JEPA~2-AC \citep{assran2025vjepa2}, a per-frame encoder (64-d) is
trained and frozen. A dynamics model is then trained on its latents. The families differ only in
how the representation is learned:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{REC}: a convolutional autoencoder trained by pixel reconstruction.
\item \textbf{JEPA}: the same encoder trained to predict the EMA-target latents of the next frames
from two past frames, with VICReg regularization \citep{bardes2022vicreg} and no decoder.
\item \textbf{STATE}: no encoder. Its inputs are ground-truth object positions, sizes, shapes and
the visible $(e,\mu)$, with no velocities and no $g$. It has perfect perception and the same
inference problem.
\end{itemize}

The dynamics model is a \emph{chunk} world model. A GRU reads the 10 standardized context latents
(and their first differences) into a belief $h_C$. An MLP decoder maps $(h_C,k)$ to the latent
$k$ steps ahead, for all $k<20$ at once. This is how video diffusion models and V-JEPA predictors
emit the future. It avoids the compounding open-loop errors that made our autoregressive pilot
models hedge toward the mean (Appendix~\ref{app:v1}).

\paragraph{Reading physics out of a model.} For REC and JEPA, predicted latents are decoded by a
pixel decoder fit \emph{post hoc} on frozen latents of held-out frames, identically for both
families. Objects are then located by soft colour-assignment centroids. On real frames this
readout is accurate to 0.7\,px for REC latents.

The \emph{effective gravity} $g_{\rm eff}$ of a prediction is the curvature of quadratic fits to
the predicted height. The fits use only those stretches where the \emph{reference} trajectory (true
or counterfactual) is in exact free flight, with a one-frame margin around contacts. Stretches are
combined by a precision-weighted median. This avoids a trap we fell into in v1: an MSE model hedges
on bounce timing and decelerates smoothly before a contact. A model-based free-flight test
mistakes that ``soft bounce'' for weak gravity. The estimator is exact on true trajectories (unit
test).

\paragraph{Three ways to measure gravity, and nested inversion (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Natural response}: $g_{\rm eff}$ of predictions from real \texttt{gsweep} contexts.
\item \textbf{Internal estimate}: a ridge probe $\hat g(h)=w^\top h+b$ fit on in-range validation
beliefs and applied to \texttt{gsweep}.
\item \textbf{Linear write}: steer $h_C\leftarrow h_C+\delta u_g$ until $\hat g$ reads a target
$g^\star$. The direction $u_g$ is estimated from \emph{matched-state counterfactual pairs}: because
conservative dynamics are time-reversible, we can build two contexts that end in \emph{exactly}
the same state but were generated under different in-range gravities. $u_g$ is therefore the change
in belief caused by gravity alone. A probe's decoding weights do not have this property
\citep{haufe2014interpretation}.
\end{itemize}

A linear write can fail for an uninteresting reason: the belief may encode $g$ along a curved
manifold. We therefore also \emph{invert} the frozen model.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{inv-$h$} optimizes the belief itself, within the GRU's range ($h=\tanh v$).
\item \textbf{inv-$z$} optimizes the ten context latents, which are passed through the frozen
belief GRU.
\end{itemize}
Both use the model's own loss on the first four predicted frames only. All metrics are computed on
the held-out continuation (frames 4--13).

The logic of the nesting:
\begin{itemize}
\item If even inv-$h$ cannot continue an out-of-range trajectory, the failure is in
\emph{execution}: no belief the dynamics can receive makes it follow $g{=}12$.
\item If inv-$h$ and inv-$z$ succeed but natural contexts do not, the failure is in
\emph{inference}: a suitable belief exists and is reachable, but real evidence does not produce it.
\item Perception is checked directly. Frames are individually in-distribution, so positions read
from OOD context latents should be as accurate as in range.
\end{itemize}

An \emph{anti-physics} control asks inversion to continue upward gravity ($g{=}-4$). If it can, the
decoder is merely flexible, and ``execution-capable'' means little.

For any response curve we report the in-range slope $s$ and the normalized out-of-range slopes
$\rho_{\rm hi}$ and $\rho_{\rm lo}$. Here $\rho{=}1$ means the law is extrapolated and $\rho{=}0$
means the curve is clamped at the training boundary.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Hypotheses, metrics, decision rules and tests were pre-registered
(\texttt{PREREGISTRATION\_v2.md}). The pre-registration was committed before any v2 model was
evaluated, and every deviation is logged there. The unit of replication is the training run: 3 seeds
for the main models and 2 for scaling cells; for REC/JEPA each seed retrains both stages. CIs are
hierarchical bootstrap intervals (seeds, then episodes; 2000 resamples). Confirmatory tests are
one-sided and Holm-corrected.
